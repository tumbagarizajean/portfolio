Improving AI-Generated Case Resolution Accuracy Through SOP Redesign

Role: Subject Matter Expert, AI Enablement · Tools: internal generative AI + Salesforce Case Management · Result: 44% → 55% resolution accuracy, 30% → 34% overall AI impact, in under two months

Describes the method and results as reported by internal performance review, without reproducing SOP content, the AI tool's internal name, or any customer/company data.

The problem

A generative AI tool was drafting resolution recommendations and reply templates for customer service cases, driven by a set of SOP-based rules. The SOP was written for a human agent to interpret, not for a rules-driven system to branch on — that gap showed up as recommendations that missed the customer's actual intent or landed on the wrong resolution template. Baseline resolution-quality was around 44%.

What I did

  1. Investigated with the people closest to the work. I coordinated directly with the agents using it — what was resolving cleanly, what wasn't, what they thought should change. The fixes came from the field, not a desk review alone.
  2. Mapped customer intent to outcome paths. For each case type, laid out the realistic set of customer intents and the correct end-to-end resolution for each — not just the happy path.
  3. Specified the logic, not the code. My contribution was the conditional scenarios — step by step, as precisely as possible — for someone else to implement, so intent was preserved through the handoff.
  4. Got ahead of edge cases before they happened. Rather than waiting for a scenario to fail and patching it, I tried to anticipate the full set of branch scenarios up front, including unlikely ones, so a gap was closed before a customer ever hit it. I credit this proactive mapping, more than any single fix, for the size of the jump.
  5. Built in checkpoints. Each branch had a validation point the tool checked before finalizing a recommendation, so an unmatched case would get flagged rather than silently mis-routed.
  6. Iterated against real outcomes. Reviewed where recommendations still missed, traced it to the ambiguous or missing branch, tightened it.

Why this worked

The core insight: an AI system is only as reliable as the decision logic behind it. Prompting or model quality wasn't the bottleneck — the SOP was. Most of the gain came from removing ambiguity a human reader would resolve unconsciously but a rules-driven system could not.

Result

MetricBeforeAfterTimeframe
SOP-driven resolution accuracy~44%~55%~2 months
Overall AI impact (combined metric)~30%~34%~2 months

Both figures were confirmed through the company's own internal performance review, not self-reported.

What I'd apply elsewhere

Any AI workflow underperforming because of vague upstream rules — not the model — benefits from the same treatment: map real-world intents first, convert guidance into explicit conditional logic, add validation checkpoints, and iterate against actual misses rather than guessing at improvements.
